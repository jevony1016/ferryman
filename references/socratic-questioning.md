# Socratic questioning (Step 0 and thinking-partner mode)

Goal: get the full picture before analysing. The questions are not small talk; each one should be able to change the conclusion.

## Rules

1. **Order by impact.** Ask first what would most change the answer. A question whose answer would not change anything is not worth asking. Information needed only later (for example a denominator for an effectiveness metric) can wait for the next round; first ask what locates the problem.
1a. **Do not assert before asking.** While questioning, do not state conclusions the user has not given grounds for ("there is inspection exposure"); ask instead.
2. **Max 3 per round.** Each with one short line on why you ask. In thinking-partner mode, ask 1 at a time.
3. **Question the question.** The user's framing often hides assumptions. "Should I take job A or B?" may hide option C, or a goal neither job serves.
4. **Follow the trigger words.** "must", "have to", "can't", "everyone", "usually", "normal people", "it's too late", "I should" → ask where it comes from and what it rests on.
5. **One layer deeper.** If an answer is still abstract ("I want stability", "I want growth"), ask what concrete thing it means: income floor? Same city? Predictable hours? Not being laid off?
6. **Do not interrogate.** Acknowledge answers briefly. If the user is tired or says "that's enough", stop and mark the rest as assumptions.
7. **No leading questions.** Do not smuggle your preferred answer into the question.

## Question bank by gap

Adapt the wording to the user's language and situation. Do not read these out mechanically.

**Goal**
- If this goes perfectly, what is different a year from now?
- What problem are you actually trying to solve: the one in front of you, or one behind it?
- Is this the goal, or a means to something else?

**Options**
- Besides these, is there an option you have ruled out without checking? Why was it ruled out?
- What would you do if neither option existed?
- Is "not deciding now" or "deciding a smaller part now" an option?

**Hard constraints**
- Which limits are physical, legal, contractual, or financial facts, and which are expectations or habits?
- Who set this deadline / rule? What happens if it slips?
- If this constraint disappeared, would the decision change? (If not, it is not doing work in the decision.)

**Stakes and reversibility**
- If you choose wrong, what exactly do you lose, and can you get it back?
- What is the cost of waiting a month to learn more?

**What is already known**
- What have you already tried, and what happened?
- Is this something you saw yourself, read, or heard from someone? Who, and what was their situation?
- Is the person giving you advice in a situation like yours?

**Preferences and values**
- What would you most regret losing?
- If money were not a factor, what would you choose? If you would still choose the same, money is not the real issue.
- Looking back from five years later, which choice would you be able to explain to yourself?
- Whose expectations are part of this decision, and do you share them?

## When the picture is complete

All six items in Step 0 are clear enough that a new answer would not change the analysis. Summarize in one or two sentences, ask the user to confirm, then move on.
