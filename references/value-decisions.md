# Value-laden decisions

Relationships, family, where to live, having children, career meaning, faith, health choices: these cannot be reduced to a number, and pretending otherwise produces false precision.

## Principles

1. **The user's values are axioms.** In these decisions the user's preferences are the base facts. Your job is to make them explicit and check the reasoning built on them, not to rank them.
2. **Separate facts from values.** "The new city's rent is X" is a fact. "I want to be near my parents" is a value. "My parents will be lonely without me" is a guess unless checked. Keep the three apart in the report.
3. **No invented scores.** No weighted matrices with made-up weights, no "7/10 happiness". If the user wants a matrix, let the user set the weights and say they are theirs.
4. **Find whose value it is.** Some "values" are someone else's expectations taken on unexamined. Ask: if no one would ever know which you chose, would you still want it?
5. **Clarify overloaded words.** "Stability", "freedom", "success", "a good life" bundle different things. Split them into concrete dimensions and ask which ones matter.

## Useful moves

- **Extreme assumptions for values**: "If money were not a factor…", "If you knew you could never undo this…", "If you were looking back at 80…". Use them to reveal priorities, then return to reality.
- **Regret test**: which choice's failure would you find easier to live with? Regret of inaction and of action feel different; name which one the user fears.
- **Small reversible trials**: try the lower-cost version before committing (spend a month working remotely from the home town; volunteer before switching careers; a trial period before moving in together).
- **Shrink what must be decided now**: often the real decision is smaller ("decide whether to apply", not "decide to move").

## What not to do

- Do not tell the user what a good person, a good son or daughter, or a successful person would do.
- Do not treat a common life script as a base fact ("by 30 you should…").
- Do not push the user toward the contrarian choice either.
- When safety, abuse, or mental health crisis is involved, step out of the method: prioritize safety and point to appropriate professional or emergency help.
